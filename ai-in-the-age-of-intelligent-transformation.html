AI in the Age of Intelligent Transformation

How to introduce AI to a workflow and measure whether it improves the outcome.

Start with one task

Consider a support team that spends time assigning incoming tickets. An AI model could suggest a category while a human confirms it. Define the allowed categories and the information the model can access before building the integration.

Measure the baseline

Record how long manual routing takes and how often tickets need reassignment. Evaluate suggestions on a held-out set that includes unclear tickets and rare categories. Measure review time and routing errors together.

Design for uncertainty

Give reviewers a way to change the category or leave a ticket unassigned. Keep a record of corrections to spot repeated failure patterns. Revisit the evaluation when the ticket mix or model changes.

Understanding the modern AI workflow

A useful workflow has an explicit purpose, a stable taxonomy and a review path. Begin by sampling real work and writing down the decisions people already make. This helps you choose an input schema the integration can reliably populate.

Use cases across industries

An operations team can prioritize warehouse exceptions while an educator can draft questions from a reading. Each deployment needs relevant samples and a different measure of value. Extra reviewer effort can cancel out gains in automation.

From demonstration to a maintained system

Put retries behind stable request IDs, log failures with a traceable model version and agree on thresholds for pausing automatic suggestions. Report how frequently reviewers overturn the recommendation.

A worked example for ticket triage

Start with a month of tickets and remove information the classifier does not need. Have reviewers label a sample independently, then inspect disagreements before treating the labels as a reference. Preserve older tickets for development and reserve a later period to simulate operational traffic.

Compare a short list of keyword rules and a model on the same held-out cases. A useful report includes per-category precision and recall, the proportion sent to manual review, and the time each reviewer spends correcting suggestions. Keep an explicit threshold for deferring an ambiguous ticket to a person.

What to document after a pilot

Publish the intended use, supported input format, baseline, evaluation period, and known blind spots. Record which issues the support team wants to address next. Before expanding to another queue, confirm that its categories and exception procedures match the assumptions of the pilot.
